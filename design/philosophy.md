# Regal Drafting

**A movement in which ornament is earned through measurement.**

Regal Drafting treats every emblem of status as an object that must first be constructed. A crown is not drawn; it is *drafted*: plotted from a lattice, braced with construction circles, annotated with dimension ticks, and only then inked. The work lives in the tension between the ceremonial and the technical. It is a blueprint of something that is normally only ever gilded. Nothing is decorative unless it can show its working.

**Space and form.** The ground is a deep, near-absolute black, and on it lies a lattice of hexagonal points. It is the quiet coordinate system from which every form is derived. The lattice is never uniform in presence. It thickens where construction is happening and dissolves into darkness where it is not, like graph paper lit by a single desk lamp. Forms are made from hairlines of one consistent weight, with vertices landing exactly on lattice points. Negative space is not emptiness but unused measurement, and it should occupy most of the canvas. The composition must feel meticulously crafted: every node placed with painstaking intention, every line terminating precisely where it must, the product of countless hours at a drafting table.

**Color and material.** There is one ink: a warm, metallic gold, used at full strength only for the final, committed line. Construction geometry, annotations and secondary structure appear in the same gold at a fraction of its opacity, so the hierarchy between *thinking* and *deciding* is carried entirely by luminance. White is reserved for the single most important word. There are no gradients for their own sake. Light arrives only as a faint, low glow behind the principal form, as if the ink itself were warm.

**Typography.** Two voices, and never more. A refined, high-contrast serif carries the one monumental gesture: large, calm, confident, set with tight and deliberate spacing. A small, widely tracked monospace carries everything else as clinical annotations, figure numbers, indices and coordinates. Its whispered labels make the piece read like a plate from an imaginary technical discipline. Text is sparse and essential. It is never explained, only placed. Every letter is positioned with master-level precision, nothing touches, and nothing crowds the margins.

**Scale, rhythm and hierarchy.** The eye travels in three steps: the serif name, the drafted emblem, and then the small print that rewards a closer look. Rhythm comes from repetition: registration marks in the corners, ticks at even intervals, the steady cadence of the lattice. The result should feel like a plate that proves something as ephemeral as ambition can be surveyed, measured and set down with care. It is quiet and exact, and unmistakably the work of someone at the top of their craft.
